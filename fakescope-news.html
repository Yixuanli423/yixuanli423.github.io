FakeScope: from image forensics to election fact-checking

Our work on FakeScope helps explain the visual evidence behind AI-generated image detection. Through our collaboration with Recod.ai at Unicamp, it is being used in VigIA to support fact-checking during Brazil’s 2026 election campaign, alongside complementary detection tools and human analysis.

The work has received coverage in Brazilian media, while the wider Recod.ai election-forensics collaboration has also featured on France 24. Below are summaries and links to the reporting, project documentation, and earlier real-world applications.

International media attention: 3 TV outlets (France 24, TV Câmara Campinas, and TV Cultura, whose coverage is forthcoming), followed by 4 online news outlets (Superinteressante, Gazeta da Amazônia, Pesquisa FAPESP, and Terra / Checamos). The television total includes 2 published programmes and 1 forthcoming report. The laboratory’s own announcements and Lupa’s methodology are additional supporting sources.

Following Anderson Rocha’s introduction, Mateus de Pádua Vicente first shows a heatmap highlighting suspicious facial regions, then demonstrates a forensic chat tool that explains individual clues: a flattened ear lacking depth, an unusually pointed shirt collar, unrealistic fabric texture, and inconsistencies in shadows and background detail. Gazeta da Amazônia4 September 2026 Election AI: tools that explain false images

Our research is helping journalists investigate AI-generated misinformation during Brazil’s 2026 elections. Within VigIA, FakeScope pairs detection scores with readable explanations of suspicious visual details, giving analysts evidence they can inspect. The initiative brings together Agência Lupa, Recod.ai, FAAP, and Meedan, combining technical analysis with human judgment.
Pesquisa FAPESPIssue 362 · April 2026 Brazilian researchers develop tools to combat disinformation

FakeScope helped investigate a fabricated video in which Lebanon’s president, Joseph Aoun, appeared to propose criminalising Hezbollah. Our model identified a distorted flag in the room and anomalies along the image edges, giving the verification team specific visual evidence to examine. These findings complemented a separate deepfake detector’s observations of changes in the watch’s appearance, unnatural facial movements, and background distortions. Featured in Pesquisa FAPESP’s coverage of the Horus project, this case shows our research contributing to a real investigation through complementary forensic evidence.
Terra / Checamos3 April 2026 Video of an explosion in a Tel Aviv building was generated by AI

FakeScope supported an Estadão Verifica investigation, published by Terra, into a video falsely presented as a bombing of a building in Tel Aviv. The video was split into 458 frames, which Mateus de Pádua Vicente analysed with our model to obtain detection results and forensic explanations. FakeScope identified light from the building that was unusually bright and uniform, lacking natural variation, and distorted perspective, with edge angles inconsistent with a real three-dimensional structure. These concrete visual clues helped fact-checkers assess the video’s synthetic origin.
